Living with lupus / Nutrition

From our heritage to our health

Emirati foods and Islamic heritage, explained through current medical knowledge.

Key message

There is no single diet that treats lupus. Balanced nutrition can support general health; kidney involvement and medicines may require individual changes. Traditional remedies must not replace prescribed care.

Explore our foods

Open each topic for a short evidence-aware explanation.

Dates

Dates provide fibre and carbohydrates. Consider portion size with diabetes; potassium restriction depends on lab results and clinical advice.

Honey

Honey can be used sparingly as a sweetener, but it is a source of sugar and is not a proven lupus treatment.

Black seed (Nigella sativa)

Mentioned in Islamic tradition, but there is insufficient clinical evidence to treat lupus. Do not use concentrated oils or extracts without consulting your clinician.

Olive oil

May replace some saturated fats as part of a balanced diet; it does not itself treat lupus.

Harees, thareed and machboos

Traditional dishes can be adapted by reducing salt and saturated fat and tailoring portions to diabetes, blood pressure and kidney health.

Important cautions

Black seed supplements, herbal mixtures and concentrated extracts should be discussed with your care team. Alfalfa sprouts and supplements are generally advised against in lupus because of reports of lupus-like immune activation. Kidney dietary restrictions are individualized, not universal.
