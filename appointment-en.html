Prepare for your appointment

Turn your questions into a clear visit plan.

A little preparation can make it easier to explain what has changed, understand decisions and leave with a clear next step.

Important

This information is educational. It does not diagnose your condition or replace advice from your healthcare team.

Key information

Before the visit

Write your three most important questions. Bring an up-to-date list of medicines and supplements, relevant results and a short record of new or changing symptoms.

Patient guide

Describe symptoms clearly

Include when each symptom began, how long it lasts, what makes it better or worse, its impact on daily life and what other symptoms occur with it.

Patient guide

During the visit

Ask for results and options to be explained in clear language. If there is more than one reasonable option, ask about benefits, harms and alternatives.

Patient guide

Before you leave

Confirm what you should do next, which tests are needed, how results will be communicated, when follow-up is due and when to contact the team earlier.

Patient guide

After the visit

Record new instructions and questions while they are fresh. Do not change medicines differently from the agreed plan without contacting your clinical team.

What should I do now?

Write down new or changing symptoms.Keep your medicines and recent results organized.Prepare your most important questions before your visit.Seek medical assessment promptly for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms.

From knowledge to action

Turn what you learned into a practical next step

Understand: What matters most from this information?Notice: What has changed or affects your daily life?Prioritize: What would you most like to improve or clarify?Discuss: What question will you take to your healthcare team?

Your goal does not have to be “control every symptom.” It can be something meaningful in daily life: more usable energy, better sleep, staying at work or study, or understanding your treatment plan.
