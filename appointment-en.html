Preparing for an appointment

Bring your life into the clinical conversation.

A useful appointment is not only a report of symptoms and test results. Prepare to explain what changed, what matters to you, what decision needs to be made, and what you need to understand before you leave.

2 · What matters to me?

Bring one life priority into the room.

Your clinician needs the medical picture, but decisions may also affect energy, sleep, work, study, family responsibilities, pregnancy plans, relationships, travel or independence. Choose what matters most right now and say it explicitly.

For example: “My biggest difficulty is having enough energy to finish my workday,” or “I want to understand how this plan fits with pregnancy in the future.”

Identify the decision before discussing the options.
3 · What are we deciding?

Identify the decision before discussing the options.

Sometimes the visit is mainly for monitoring. At other times there is a decision: whether to start, continue or reconsider a treatment; whether a symptom needs investigation; or how follow-up should change. Ask what decision, if any, needs to be made today.

Ask about benefits, harms, alternatives and uncertainty.
4 · What are my options?

Ask about benefits, harms, alternatives and uncertainty.

If there is more than one reasonable option, ask how they differ and how each might fit your circumstances and priorities. General information cannot determine which option is right for you.

What benefit are we aiming for?What are the important harms or burdens?What alternatives are reasonable?What happens if we wait or do not change the plan now?

5 · What did we agree?

Use your own words before you leave.

Explain the plan back in your own words: what you will do, what will be monitored, and when the plan will be reviewed. If your understanding is incomplete, ask for clarification. This is not a test of you; it is a way to check that the explanation was clear.

Leave with a follow-up plan, not just information.

Safety

Appointment preparation is not a reason to delay urgent care.

Severe or rapidly worsening symptoms require medical assessment rather than waiting for a scheduled visit or using a website tool. Follow any individualized urgent-care instructions already given by your healthcare team.
