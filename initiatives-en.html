UAE Lupus Patient Advisory Network

NA7N UAE Lupus Patient Advisory Network (PAN)

A structured network where people living with lupus bring lived experience into research, from the first question through to what findings mean for patients. Inspired by the operational model of LUPUS EUROPE's PAN, with an independent UAE identity and governance.

The mission

Connect the UAE lupus community with researchers, clinicians and study developers through trained patient advisors. Advisors bring lived experience to make research projects, information and eventual treatment pathways more relevant to patients. Their role is advisory, not a substitute for researchers, doctors or research ethics committees.

Following the same advisory functions

What does a patient advisor do?

Once established, research teams may request appropriate patient involvement at different stages of a project, subject to agreements and approvals.

01

Shape research questions

Help determine whether a study addresses questions and problems that matter to patients.

02

Review study and trial design

Comment on visit burden, practical participation and meaningful outcomes, without making scientific or clinical decisions for researchers.

03

Review participant information and consent

Help ensure documents use language patients can understand before deciding whether to participate.

04

Join research project discussions

Contribute patient perspectives to appropriate research meetings or advisory groups under a clear agreement.

05

Explain findings in accessible language

Help prepare plain-language, including Arabic, research summaries after scientific review.

06

Identify barriers to participation and access

Share patient perspectives on practical barriers in research, information and care, without giving individual medical advice.

Proposed operating structure

How UAE PAN would operate

A structured advisory team rather than an open group chat or a formally registered association by default.

Patient advisors

A target group of 20–25 adults living with lupus in the UAE, with varied experiences, Emirates and languages. This is a future goal, not a current membership count.

Network coordinator

A point of contact for research requests, matching advisors with specific projects and documenting outcomes without collecting unnecessary health information.

Research collaborators

Researchers, universities and healthcare institutions may eventually request advice under clear agreements and approvals. No signed partnerships are announced.

Governance and review

A code of conduct, confidentiality where necessary, conflicts-of-interest declarations, informed agreement and a right to decline or withdraw.

Participation pathway — once operational

From patient to research advisor

  1. 01Invitation and fair selection

    Once secure recruitment opens, adults may express interest. Clear selection criteria should value lived experience, not just scientific training.

  2. 02Foundational training

    Introduction to clinical research, ethics, study protocols, consent documents, constructive communication and confidentiality.

  3. 03Matching to a project

    Each advisor receives a defined task, expected time commitment, disclosure of potential compensation and conflicts, and can choose whether to participate.

  4. 04Documented input and feedback

    Advisors give comments; the research team explains which suggestions were used and why, with non-identifying aggregate reporting.

Who could become an advisor?

The intended members are adults living with lupus and residing in the UAE who can respectfully represent perspectives beyond their own. A medical or research qualification is not required: lived experience is the core expertise. Participation should be flexible when fatigue or symptom fluctuations affect availability.

How would a researcher request advice?

Once established, a research team would provide a short project brief, the purpose of seeking patient input, scope, time commitment, privacy arrangements and relevant approvals. The network would then coordinate advisor matching and document outcomes. This service is not currently active.

Safeguards and limits

  • This page does not collect medical reports, diagnoses, identity documents or applications.
  • The network will not run clinical trials or collect research data without a qualified partner and appropriate ethical and regulatory approvals.
  • An advisory role is not individual clinical advice and does not automatically speak for every person with lupus.
  • Relevant volunteering, privacy and licensing requirements must be assessed for each Emirate before operations begin.

Current programme status

In formation — no member recruitment yet

NA7N has prepared this proposed framework and public information page. The advisor group has not yet been recruited or trained, and no research partnerships are being claimed. Operations require a responsible organisation and the relevant approvals.

What is required to launch

  1. Identify a qualified operating organisation or licensed partner and programme lead.
  2. Approve a charter, scope of work, independence policy and conflict-of-interest process.
  3. Establish a secure and legally appropriate recruitment and consent pathway.
  4. Approve advisor training, selection criteria and a research-request process.
  5. Check research ethics and regulatory approvals for each project where required.

Source model and independence

This independent UAE proposal adopts the patient-advisory function of LUPUS EUROPE's PAN. It is not a branch, partner, or endorsed programme of LUPUS EUROPE; these descriptions are original local programme materials.
