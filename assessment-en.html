Q-SLAQ — Lupus symptoms over the past month

This questionnaire asks about lupus-related symptoms and your perception of disease activity during the past month. It reports three separate measures, not a validated quality-of-life score or a medical diagnosis.

Validation and use status

The scoring algorithm follows the Swedish Rheumatology Quality Register. Digital use and translation rights are under review. This version is for testing with fictitious answers only, not patient assessment or treatment decisions.

Understanding the results

What do these scores mean?

NA7N check-in

Three ratings describe the impact of fatigue, sleep and health on daily function; the fourth asks about overall quality of life. The directions of the scales differ, so averaging them is not a validated quality-of-life score.

Q-SLAQ

Higher symptom-related scores mean more or more severe symptoms were reported. They do not establish that lupus is medically more active, or prove that lupus caused every symptom.

Use the trend in context

Track meaningful changes, prepare questions and review them with your care team rather than making treatment decisions from a single number.

Test phase and privacy

Where do the answers go?

NA7N check-ins are submitted to your account only after sign-in. The current Supabase environment is for fictitious test records; do not use it for real patient responses. Q-SLAQ results are calculated in the browser and are not automatically added as a clinical assessment to the Supabase account. Optional local-device storage, if offered, requires separate consent and a passphrase.

What should I do next?

You can try either questionnaire with fictitious answers, then use the insights to understand how patient-reported information can support a clinical conversation. New or severe chest pain, major breathlessness, neurological symptoms or rapid deterioration require clinical assessment instead of online self-scoring.
