Symptoms and health changes

Notice the change. Describe the pattern. Do not diagnose yourself from one symptom.

When something feels different, your job is not to decide whether lupus is active. Focus on what changed, how it affects your life, what happened around it, and what your healthcare team needs to know.

Three things to remember

Lupus symptoms vary from person to person. A symptom can have more than one possible cause. A new symptom does not automatically mean a lupus flare, so do not change treatment based on symptoms alone.

Safety first

Seek urgent assessment for severe, sudden or rapidly worsening symptoms. A website cannot determine their cause. View relevant guidance

Understand, do not self-diagnose

Could it be lupus? Possibly—but that is not the only question.

Fatigue, joint symptoms, skin changes, fever and other symptoms can occur with lupus, but they can also have other causes. Infection, medicines and unrelated health conditions may produce similar changes. Your symptom record supports clinical assessment; it does not replace it.

How is this affecting your life?

The same symptom can matter differently to different people. Fatigue that prevents you from working, pain that interrupts sleep, or a rash that changes what you can do outdoors are meaningful outcomes to discuss—not just symptoms to count.

Choose the one or two changes that are having the greatest effect on your day. That helps make the conversation with your healthcare team more focused.

Safety

Know when a website is not the right tool.

New severe chest pain, significant or sudden breathing difficulty, new severe neurological symptoms, seizures, fainting, severe confusion, major bleeding, or rapid and concerning deterioration require medical assessment rather than website self-assessment. Follow any individualized urgent-care instructions already given by your healthcare team.

For new, persistent or clearly worsening symptoms that are not an emergency, contact your healthcare team rather than assuming they are a flare or changing treatment yourself.
