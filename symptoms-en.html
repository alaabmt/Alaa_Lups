Symptoms

Understand the pattern, not just one symptom.

Lupus symptoms vary widely. A symptom that is common in lupus does not prove lupus, and a new symptom in someone with lupus does not automatically mean the disease is active.

Important

This information is educational. It does not diagnose your condition or replace advice from your healthcare team.

Key information

Common symptom groups

Possible symptoms include fatigue, fever, joint pain or swelling, skin rashes, sensitivity to ultraviolet light, hair loss and mouth sores. Lupus can also affect organs such as the kidneys, heart, lungs, blood or nervous system.

Patient guide

How to describe a new symptom

Record when it started, whether it is new or recurring, how long it lasts, how severe it is, how it affects daily activities, what other symptoms occur with it, and whether medicines, infection, sun exposure or other health changes have occurred.

Patient guide

Fatigue

Fatigue can be related to lupus, but it can also have other causes such as anemia, sleep problems, infection, medication effects, thyroid disorders or other conditions.

Patient guide

Joint symptoms

Lupus may cause joint pain, stiffness or swelling. Because joint pain has many causes, symptoms alone cannot identify the cause.

Patient guide

When to seek help

New severe chest pain, significant shortness of breath, new neurological symptoms, severe confusion, seizures or rapid deterioration need prompt medical assessment rather than self-assessment through a website.

What should I do now?

Write down new or changing symptoms.Keep your medicines and recent results organized.Prepare your most important questions before your visit.Seek medical assessment promptly for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms.
