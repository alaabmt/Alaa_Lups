Patient stories

Every experience deserves a voice and a safe space.

We hope to publish authentic first-person stories from people living with lupus—about daily challenges, support, resilience and finding what matters. Individual experiences are never presented as medical advice.

Submissions are not yet open

We are establishing privacy safeguards, consent procedures, secure storage and editorial review. Please do not submit your medical story through our other site forms or social media for publication until a dedicated, approved channel is announced.

What could your story be about?

A day with lupus

Work, study, fatigue, relationships and planning your day.

What helped me cope

Support networks, preparing questions and speaking with your care team.

What I wish others knew

An honest, personal perspective without general treatment recommendations.

How stories will be reviewed

  1. Voluntary submission through an approved secure channel; adults only in the initial phase.
  2. Privacy screening to remove identifying details of other people.
  3. Editing that preserves the writer's voice, with clinical-safety escalation when needed.
  4. The final version is returned to the author for documented, specific publication consent; separate decisions cover real names, photos, translation and social sharing.
  5. Final publication approval, with a process for corrections and removal requests.

Published stories

No patient stories have been published in this section yet. We will not present fictional examples as real patient testimonials.

Privacy and your rights

Participation is optional and does not affect access to educational content. When submissions open, the process will explain the purpose of collection, retention, responsible organisation and rights contact before any health details are entered. A pseudonym cannot guarantee anonymity where the story itself is identifying. We will also explain that external copies or shares may remain after a story is removed.

Health note: Personal stories do not prove that a treatment works or replace individual medical advice. Do not use this area for emergencies.
