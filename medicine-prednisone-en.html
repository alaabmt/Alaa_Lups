Corticosteroids (prednisone)

Can reduce inflammation relatively quickly but long-term or high-dose use has important risks.

Why might it be prescribed?

May be used for flares or organ inflammation while other treatments work toward long-term control.

What makes this medicine different?

They work more broadly than NSAID pain relievers and are not automatically a permanent substitute for longer-term disease control.

Before adding any medicineAsk your pharmacist to check interactions, including OTC medicines and supplements. Do not change treatment based on this page.

Explore the topics that matter to you

How it works: analogy and explanation
Imagine emergency brakes reducing a vehicle's speed quickly. Steroids can help urgently, but the clinician guides how the dose is safely reduced.

They affect many inflammatory and immune pathways and the body's own hormone system.

The analogy is a learning aid, not a literal description of biology.

How is it used and when may it help?

Response may be rapid, but dose, schedule and duration depend on your prescription and clinical situation.

If you miss a dose, follow the instructions for your specific product or ask your pharmacist. Do not double up without explicit advice.

Side effects and monitoring

Appetite changes, sleep disruption, mood changes, and higher glucose or blood pressure may occur.

Do not stop abruptly on your own; tapering may be required. Ask about infection, blood pressure, glucose and bone health monitoring.

Which medicines may interact?

Using ibuprofen or naproxen may increase stomach bleeding risk. Review blood thinners, vaccines and other medicines with your clinician.

These are important examples, not a complete interaction list. Some combinations are intentional with monitoring; others may need avoidance. Bring your complete medicine list to your appointments.

Pregnancy, breastfeeding and family planning

May be used during pregnancy when needed, with individualized risk-benefit assessment.

When to get prompt or urgent medical help

Seek urgent assessment for vomiting blood, black stools, severe infection, dangerous mood changes or marked weakness after abrupt discontinuation.

How does it compare with other medicines?
NSAIDs treat some symptoms but cannot replace steroids in important organ inflammation; hydroxychloroquine commonly works over longer periods.
