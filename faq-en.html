Frequently asked questions

Clear answers to common lupus questions.

These answers provide general education and do not replace assessment by your healthcare team.

Important

This information is educational. It does not diagnose your condition or replace advice from your healthcare team.

Key information

Is there one test that confirms lupus?

No. Lupus diagnosis uses the overall clinical picture. ANA is important but a positive ANA alone does not confirm lupus.

Patient guide

Does every new symptom mean a flare?

No. Symptoms can have many causes, including infection, medicine effects or other health conditions. New or worsening symptoms should be considered in context.

Patient guide

Can lupus affect the kidneys without pain?

Yes. Kidney involvement may first appear through urine or blood-test changes, which is one reason regular monitoring can be important.

Patient guide

Should I stop medicine when I feel better?

Do not stop or change prescribed lupus medicines on your own. Improvement may reflect effective treatment, and stopping some medicines can create risk.

Patient guide

Is there a diet that cures lupus?

There is no established diet that cures lupus. Nutrition advice may need to be individualized for kidney disease, cardiovascular risk, medicines or other conditions.

Patient guide

Can people with lupus become pregnant?

Many can, but pre-pregnancy planning is important. Disease activity, medicines, kidney health and certain antibodies may affect pregnancy management.

What should I do now?

Write down new or changing symptoms.Keep your medicines and recent results organized.Prepare your most important questions before your visit.Seek medical assessment promptly for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms.
