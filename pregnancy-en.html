Pregnancy & lupus

Plan pregnancy with your healthcare team before you conceive.

Many people with lupus can have successful pregnancies, but planning matters because disease activity, organ involvement, antibodies and medicines can affect pregnancy care.

Important

This information is educational. It does not diagnose your condition or replace advice from your healthcare team.

Key information

Plan before pregnancy

Discuss pregnancy before conception when possible. Your team may want to review disease activity, kidney health, blood pressure, relevant antibodies and current medicines.

Patient guide

Review medicines early

Some medicines may be continued in pregnancy while others may need to be changed well before conception. Do not stop a prescribed medicine on your own; medication decisions should be made with the clinicians managing your lupus and pregnancy.

Patient guide

Disease control matters

Pregnancy planning is generally safer when lupus is well controlled. Individual timing depends on your health and should be discussed with your specialist team.

Patient guide

Special monitoring

Some patients need additional monitoring because of kidney disease, antiphospholipid antibodies, anti-Ro/SSA or anti-La/SSB antibodies, hypertension or other factors.

Patient guide

Urgent symptoms

Severe headache, chest pain, significant shortness of breath, heavy bleeding or other acute concerning symptoms during pregnancy require prompt medical assessment.

What should I do now?
