Diagnosis & tests

A test result is one piece of the picture.

Lupus is diagnosed and monitored by combining symptoms, examination findings and laboratory information. Do not interpret one result in isolation.

Important

This information is educational. It does not diagnose your condition or replace advice from your healthcare team.

Key information

ANA

Antinuclear antibody (ANA) is positive in most people with lupus, but it can also be positive in people who do not have lupus. A positive ANA alone does not establish the diagnosis.

Patient guide

Blood tests

A complete blood count can help monitor red cells, white cells and platelets. Other blood tests may help clinicians assess inflammation, kidney function, antibodies or complement proteins depending on the clinical question.

Patient guide

Urine and kidney monitoring

Urinalysis and urine protein measurements can help identify kidney involvement. Kidney problems can sometimes be present without pain or obvious symptoms.

Patient guide

Trends matter

Reference ranges can differ between laboratories. Clinicians interpret your current result alongside previous results, symptoms, examination, medicines and other tests.

Patient guide

Useful questions

Ask: Why was this test ordered? Is this result new? How does it compare with my previous results? Does it need repeating? What should I watch for before my next visit?
