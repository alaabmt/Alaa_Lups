Kidneys & lupus

Kidney involvement can be quiet, which is why monitoring matters.

Lupus nephritis is inflammation of the kidneys related to lupus. Changes can sometimes appear in urine or blood tests before a person feels obvious kidney symptoms.

Important

This information is educational. It does not diagnose your condition or replace advice from your healthcare team.

Key information

Why urine tests matter

Urinalysis and urine protein measurements can detect changes that may suggest kidney involvement. Results need interpretation in clinical context.

Patient guide

Kidney function

Blood tests such as creatinine help clinicians assess kidney function, but no single number tells the whole story.

Patient guide

Kidney biopsy

In selected situations, a kidney biopsy may help determine the type and severity of kidney inflammation and guide treatment decisions.

Patient guide

Treatment and monitoring

Treatment depends on the pattern and severity of kidney involvement. Follow-up may include urine, blood pressure, kidney function and other laboratory monitoring.

Patient guide

What to ask

Ask whether your urine or kidney results have changed, what trend your team is following, how often monitoring is needed and which symptoms should prompt earlier contact.
